## 5. NEGLIGENCE ARGUMENTS

### Argument 1: Protocol Deviation — Dosing Into Known Renal Decline
**Strength: VERY STRONG**

- Jan 4 eGFR of 53 was reviewed as pre-dose assessment (confirmed by lab record notes)
- **Jan 4 CRP was 17.93 mg/dL (60x normal) — massive systemic inflammation visible on the same labs** (NEW)
- Standard clinical trial protocol for nephrotoxic agents includes eGFR <60 as a Hold/Discontinuation criterion
- jRCT registry confirms "adequate organ function" was a named enrollment criterion (#8) — eGFR 53 + CRP 60x normal is not "adequate" (NEW)
- Potassium supplementation started Jan 13 — before dose 7 on Jan 18 — proves documented awareness of renal impact
- Dose 7 administered Jan 18 with eGFR at 44 and CRP 20.78 (69x normal) — both worsening (NEW)
- Trial completed to 8th dose on Jan 28 with eGFR in the 30s
- **This was a Phase 1 safety trial whose primary endpoint was dose-limiting toxicity — the investigators were specifically tasked with catching exactly this kind of safety signal** (NEW)
- **NCCHE was simultaneously sponsor and investigator — no independent party was monitoring their dosing decisions** (NEW)
- **Required evidence:** Original trial protocol document — specifically the Discontinuation/Hold criteria section and the definition of "adequate organ function" from inclusion criterion #8

### Argument 2: Failure to Refer to Nephrologist — 37-Day Gap
**Strength: VERY STRONG**

- Jan 26: Discharged with eGFR 26, kidneys noted as "swollen." No nephrology referral.
- Feb 5–Mar 2: Six outpatient visits. eGFR declining. No referral.
- Feb 20: Blood transfusion administered outpatient — admission-level intervention
- Mar 3: First nephrology contact → **immediate admission ordered**
- The immediate admission order is documentary proof the patient required inpatient specialist care weeks earlier
- Admission delayed only by bed availability (not clinical judgment) — nephrologist's intent to admit was same-day
- **37-day gap between confirmed Stage 4 failure and first specialist contact is legally indefensible for a Special Function Hospital**

### Argument 3: Inadequate Outpatient Treatment — Visibly Failing Protocol
**Strength: STRONG**

- Predonine started at 5mg/day (Jan 28) — conservative for a patient with active AIN
- Escalated to 10mg/day (Feb 27) only after weeks of visible failure
- eGFR trajectory during treatment: 26→25→20→20→18 — continuous decline
- No hospitalization despite blood transfusion requirement (Feb 20)
- No escalation to pulse therapy until Mar 18 — 51+ days after injury confirmed
- CRP remained massively elevated throughout, indicating uncontrolled inflammation

### Argument 4: Loss of Chance
**Strength: STRONG (requires expert testimony)**

- Medical literature supports early steroid intervention for drug/contrast-induced AIN
- Pulse therapy at eGFR 26 (Jan 26) would have been "within window"
- Pulse therapy at eGFR 13.6 (Mar 18) — minimal response
- Mar 25 uptick to 15.7 proves residual renal function existed — earlier treatment had realistic chance of preserving more
- CRP still 70x normal post-pulse — inflammation was never controlled during outpatient period
- **Requires nephrologist expert opinion to formally bridge delay → permanent damage**
